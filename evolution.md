## The evolution of Decision Making  

From rigid numerical scoring methods to linguistic term approaches and text mining. More to come under the **Large Language Models**.

### **1. Quantitative Models**
- Decision-making is rooted in **operations research** and **economics**, relying heavily on numerical models.
- **Multi-Criteria Decision Making (MCDM)** (e.g., AHP by Saaty, 1970s) assigned **numerical scores** to options based on weighted criteria.

### **2. Linguistic Terms and Fuzzy Decision Making**
- Researchers began integrating **linguistic variables** and **fuzzy logic** (Zadeh, 1965).
- Decision-makers often describe preferences using terms like *high, medium, low* rather than exact numbers.
- Methods like **Fuzzy AHP**, **Fuzzy TOPSIS**, and **Fuzzy Delphi** were developed to translate vague human judgments into decision models.
- Linguistic decision-making found applications in areas like expert systems, qualitative risk assessment, and preference modeling.

### **3. Mining Structured Text and Semi-Structured Data**
- Decision-making became more data-driven.
- Structured data sources (e.g., databases, XML, spreadsheets) could be systematically analyzed.
- Techniques like **Natural Language Processing (NLP)** and **Text Mining** were used to analyze structured textual sources.
- Decision Support Systems (DSS) and Business Intelligence (BI) tools started integrating structured text analysis.

### **4. Mining Free-Form Text and Unstructured Data (2010s–Present)**
- Under the rise of **big data** sources shift towards **unstructured textual data**, such as emails, news articles, social media, and open-ended survey responses.
- Sentiment analysis, topic modeling (e.g., LDA, BERT), and deep learning models (e.g., transformers) enabled automatic extraction of decision-making insights from raw text.
- Multi-modal decision support emerged, combining numerical scores, linguistic evaluations, structured databases, and free-form text insights.
- New use of **LLMs (e.g., GPT-based models)** to assist in qualitative and quantitative decision analysis.

Decision-making research has evolved from rigid numerical methods to flexible, AI-powered text mining approaches, enhancing the ability to handle uncertainty, ambiguity, and vast amounts of data.
